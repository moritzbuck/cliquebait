# CliqueBait : detecting clades living at the edge (of the 95% ANI gap)

A 95% Average Nucleotide Identity is widely accepted as a threshold to operationally define bacterial species. It is surprisingly robust to observation and no good counter example was known until a large amount of Prochlorochoccus were sequenced. The observation that some clades of Prochlorococcus broke this barrier made the development of a tool to find such clade both easier and a necessity.
CliqueBait is just such a tool. It searches for cliques inside of the ANI network formed by pairwise comparison of the genomes. Where a clique is a set of of nodes in a network were all the nodes are connected to each other. Searching for cliques rapidely degenerates to an NP-hard problem. This is circumvented with the use of a guide tree used to reduce the number of possible cliques to the set of nodes of that tree. This set is then parsed iteratively from the tips to find break points in clique ANI (the maximum ANI at which the set of genomes forms a clique).
CliqueBait uses pairwise ANIs of a set of genomes as imput and outputs clusters of genomes, as well as a number of diagnostic statistics and figures. It can not only detect clusters of genomes that extent out over the 95% ANI boundary but also clades that are completely over the boundary (such as some Prochlorococcus), where there ANI of all members are lower than 95%. While these clades could be argued to be genuses-level clades where each genome represents a different species some of the clusters obtained while running CliqueBait on the genomes referenced in GTDB had more than hundred members (some of which associated with the human microbiome, such as Rothia and Haemophilus). We hypothesise that some of these clusters could be clades that do not have any mechanisms for homologous recombinations. Further study of such "pseudo-species" could give us invaluable insight into the nature of "species" and the 95% ANI boundary, and the evolutionary forces conserving them. And in the context of this conference, the impact on pangenome structure of species with low/no homologous recombination. An alpha version of CliqueBait can be found at https://github.com/moritzbuck/cliquebait.

# CliqueBait : detecting clades living at the edge (of the 95% ANI gap)

A 95% Average Nucleotide Identity (ANI) is widely accepted as a good operational threshold for delineating bacterial species. This boundary has proven surprisingly robust, with very few exceptions reported, until the large-scale sequencing of Prochlorococcus. Some Prochlorococcus clades were found to cross this threshold, revealing groups where members share <95% ANI while still forming genomically coherent species. This observation highlights the need for systematic tools to detect such clades.

CliqueBait is designed to meet this need. It identifies cliques within an ANI network derived from pairwise genome comparisons, where a clique is defined as a set of nodes in which every genome is connected to every other genome. Because max-clique detection is computationally NP-hard, CliqueBait uses a guide tree to restrict the search space to subsets of genomes defined along the tree. These subsets are then parsed iteratively from the tips inward to identify breakpoints in clique ANI (the maximum ANI at which a set of genomes still forms a clique).

CliqueBait takes pairwise ANI values as input and outputs clusters of genomes along with diagnostic statistics and visualizations. It can detect both cliques that extend beyond the canonical 95% ANI boundary and clades entirely below that threshold, such as those seen in Prochlorococcus, where all pairwise ANI values fall under 95%. While such clades could be interpreted as genus-level groups in which each genome represents a distinct species, some clusters detected in GTDB-referenced genomes contain over 100 members, including human-associated taxa such as Rothia and Haemophilus.

We hypothesize that these unusually large, low-ANI clades may lack mechanisms for homologous recombination, representing “pseudo-species” whose boundaries are maintained by evolutionary forces distinct from those often proposed to explain the 95% ANI gap. Further investigation of these clades may provide important insight into the nature of bacterial species concepts, the evolutionary underpinnings of the 95% ANI threshold, and, particularly relevant to this conference, the impact of limited recombination on pangenome structure.

An alpha version of CliqueBait is available at: https://github.com/moritzbuck/cliquebait
